Terms of service

How we scope, price and hand over work, who owns what, and what happens if something goes wrong. Short, because it should be readable.

Last updated: 2026-11-05. These terms cover work we do for you through www.theflowlabs.online. They are written to be read, not skimmed past. If anything here conflicts with a signed proposal or statement of work, the signed document wins.

1. Scoping comes before code

Every project starts with a written scope: what is being built, what is explicitly out of scope, what you provide, and what "done" means. We do not start build work on a verbal understanding. If the scope changes after you approve it, we re-quote the delta before doing the work, in writing, so there is never a surprise invoice at the end.

2. Fixed price, paid in milestones

  • The quote is the price for the agreed scope. It does not move because we underestimated our own effort.
  • Payment is split into milestones, typically a deposit to begin, one or two payments tied to reviewable stages, and the balance on handover. The split is written into the proposal before you pay anything.
  • Invoices are due on the terms stated on the invoice. Work pauses on overdue invoices, and resumes when they clear. We would rather tell you that plainly than keep building and stay quiet about it.
  • Third-party costs are yours and are passed through at cost — domains, hosting, paid APIs, fonts, licences. We tell you which ones a project needs before we start.

3. You own the work on handover

When the final invoice is paid, you own the deliverables we built for you: the source code, the design files, the content we wrote for you and the documentation. We hand over the repository and the credentials, not a locked box.

Two honest exceptions: third-party components keep their own licences (open-source libraries, fonts, stock assets), and our pre-existing tooling — internal helpers, starter templates and patterns we reuse across projects — stays ours, licensed to you for use inside what we built. We will always say which is which.

Unless you ask us not to, we may show the finished work in our portfolio and describe it in a case study. We will not publish your metrics, your customers or anything confidential without your written approval.

4. What we need from you

  • One decision-maker who can approve scope, design and final sign-off.
  • Content, access and feedback in reasonable time. Timelines slip when input stalls; we will flag it early rather than absorb it silently.
  • Accurate information about your business, and the right to share any data you give us.

5. Timeline and revisions

Delivery dates are estimates unless the proposal states a fixed date. Each project includes the revision rounds listed in the scope; further rounds are quoted separately. Once a stage is approved, reopening it counts as a scope change.

6. Support after handover

Every project includes a warranty window (stated in the proposal) for defects in what we built: things that do not work as specified. It does not cover new features, third-party outages, or changes made by someone else. After the window, support is available on a quoted basis, and you are never locked in — the code is yours.

7. Limits of liability

We deliver the work described in the scope, with reasonable skill and care. We are not liable for indirect or consequential losses, lost profits, or lost data beyond our control. Our total liability for any project is capped at the fees paid for that project. Keep your own backups; we will help you set them up.

8. Cancellation

Either side can end a project in writing. You pay for the milestones completed and work in progress up to that point, and you receive what has been paid for. The deposit is not refundable once build work has started, because it reserves studio time that cannot be resold.

9. Governing law

These terms are governed by the laws of India. Disputes are subject to the exclusive jurisdiction of the courts at Bhubaneswar, Odisha. Before anything formal, talk to us: every dispute we have had so far was a conversation.

10. Changes to these terms

We may update this page as the studio changes. The date at the top changes with it. The terms that apply to your project are the ones in effect when you approve the proposal.

Questions

Anything unclear, ask before you sign: hello@theflowlabs.online. We would rather spend ten minutes explaining a clause than have you agree to something you do not understand.
