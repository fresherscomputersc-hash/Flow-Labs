How to plan a website for a new startup.

Goals, pages, copy, budget and the classic traps startups fall into with their first website. A practical plan you can actually follow this week.

By The Flow Labs Engineering Team · Updated November 2026

Most startup websites fail before a single line of code is written, because nobody decided what the website is actually FOR. Not the all-hands committee decisions — the practical commercial ones. Here is the operational plan we wish every founder walked in with.

Step 1: Choose ONE primary commercial job

A website can pull off multiple secondary tasks, but it is exponentially stronger when one job is primary:

  • Collect qualified leads: The primary objective for B2B service studios and consultancies.
  • Convert self-service signups: Crucial for product-stage SaaS apps and early betas.
  • Convince angel/institutional investors: Essential in early fundraising cycles where technical credibility is everything.
  • Establish professional legitimacy: Ensuring visitors get immediate proof when Googling your brand name after a meeting.

Step 2: The essential pages you actually need

  • Home: What you build, who it is for, and one obvious next step. If a visitor cannot tell what you do within 5 seconds, nothing else matters.
  • Proof: Real case studies with verifiable labels. Proof beats promises every day of the week. (See how our work showroom classifies live, in-house, and open-source systems).
  • Services / Product: A concrete, transparent offer rather than vague generic agency buzzwords.
  • Scoping / Contact: A streamlined intake form that routes directly to decision-makers.
"Nine-page drop-down menus are distraction. One primary page, one focused job, and one clear call to action."

Step 3: Write for the customer, not for the founder

The most common trap is writing pages that describe what the founder finds fascinating, while ignoring what the customer needs to know. Every sentence must either help a prospect decide or be deleted.

Step 4: Realistic budget expectations

DIY website builders are cheap up front, but produce slow-loading bloat and poor mobile experiences. Large agencies bill high overhead for account managers. Founder-led studios like ours deliver fixed pricing, sub-second performance, and clean repository ownership.

Step 5: Pre-launch quality verification

  1. Renders fast on a budget 4G mobile device without layout shifts.
  2. All forms transmit cleanly to a database with zero dropped inquiries.
  3. SEO canonicals, Open Graph cards, and structured JSON-LD schemas are indexed.
  4. Every interactive button and touchpoint works without error.
